What is Qoax Community?

Qoax Community is an independent non-profit technology community based in Sofia, Bulgaria. We are engineers, teachers, and students who believe that good technology should be measured by what it leaves behind: knowledge, confidence, and local capacity. That is why we work in the open, with schools and organisations that will keep the results long after we step back.

Most of our work is practical and unglamorous: mentoring an internship cohort, keeping a theatre installation running, building a website an NGO can maintain on its own, or helping a student council put on its first hackathon. We document each of these as a public record under Events and Our work.

Коакс Комюнити е независимо сдружение с нестопанска цел в София. Организираме хакатони, гейм джамове и турнири с училища и университети и разработваме софтуер за граждански и културни организации.

The point is not to look innovative. The point is to leave people with more skill, more confidence, and something useful that did not exist before.

Qoax Community, founding note

How we work

  • Build

    Useful software shaped around the people who have to live with it after launch.

  • Teach

    Project-led learning that leaves students with transferable skill and visible work.

  • Support

    Practical time, tools, and technical care for student, cultural, and civic initiatives.

  • Publish

    Honest notes that keep the constraints, mistakes, recoveries, and decisions visible.

All questions

What is Qoax Community?

Qoax Community is an independent non-profit technology community based in Sofia, Bulgaria. We run hackathons, game jams, and tournaments with schools and universities, mentor students through the summer internship programme, and build software for civic and cultural organisations.

Is Qoax a company?

No. Qoax Community is a registered non-profit association, UIC (ЕИК) 208896893, registered in Sofia on 24 July 2026. There is no commercial catalogue and no advertising on this site.

What events does Qoax run?

In the 2026–2027 season: Atanasoff48, a 48-hour hackathon at SPGE John Atanasoff on 2–4 October 2026; the John Atanasoff Game Jam planned for December 2026; the Telecommunications School Hackathon planned for March–April 2027; the completed FMI Game Jam at the Faculty of Mathematics and Informatics, Sofia University, with photos coming in the next few days; the Qoax × SUGAMING Gaming Tournament in early March 2027; and the summer Internship Programme for students from partner schools. Exact dates and participation details for the two planned school events will be announced once confirmed.

Do you build software for NGOs and artists?

Yes, for public-interest organisations: NGO websites, theatre installations, and civic tools. We build things the organisation can keep running after we step back, and we publish each as a record. See our work.

Have a public-interest project that needs practical technology support?

Write to us with what you are trying to do. We reply to every message.
